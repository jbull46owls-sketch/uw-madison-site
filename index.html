Built on a sandbar
between two lakes.

Lake Mendota to the north, Lake Monona to the south. The city of Madison and the University of Wisconsin sit on the four-mile strip of land between them — an isthmus — and the campus is shaped by the water on both sides of it.

Geography

Water on both sides of nearly every walk to class

Most college towns have a river, or a lake on the edge of things. Madison has two lakes pressed against a strip of land narrow enough to bike across in fifteen minutes. Bascom Hill looks down toward Lake Mendota; walk the other direction from the Capitol and you're at Lake Monona in ten minutes.

The university's oldest buildings line Mendota's shore because the lake was the reason the site was chosen in 1848. Nothing about that has changed — the Memorial Union Terrace still sits right at the waterline, and student sailboats still launch a few hundred feet from the library.

Living here

A campus that actually has four seasons

Fall — Bascom Hill turns, Camp Randall fills

The maples on Bascom Hill turn before the first home football game, and on Saturdays the whole isthmus routes around Camp Randall Stadium and 80,000 people in red.

Winter — the lake freezes and people walk on it

Once Lake Mendota's ice is thick enough, ice-fishing shanties and pickup hockey games show up on the surface a short walk from the dorms. The Terrace chairs get stacked; everyone moves indoors to the Rathskeller until spring.

Spring — the Terrace reopens

The Memorial Union Terrace's sunburst chairs come back out the first warm week, and the lake fills with sailboats again as the student-run Hoofers sailing club reopens the docks.

Summer — the Capitol Square becomes a market

Saturday mornings, the Dane County Farmers' Market wraps the entire Capitol Square, and the Terrace runs concerts most nights through August — both a few minutes from campus.

Getting around

Half a mile, no car required

State Street runs in a straight, closed-to-cars line from the steps of the Capitol to the steps of Bascom Hall. It's the spine of the city: restaurants, the Wisconsin Union, music venues, and the used bookstores students actually use, all inside a fifteen-minute walk.

Most students never need a car for four years — class, groceries, the lake, and downtown are all reachable on foot or by bike, which is unusual for a campus this size.
